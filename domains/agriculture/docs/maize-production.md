About this maize guide: this advice comes from the IITA "Guide to Maize Production in Northern Nigeria" (2020), written for the savanna zones of northern Nigeria (the project worked in Borno and Adamawa states). Farmers in other parts of Nigeria should ask their state ADP (Agricultural Development Programme) extension office.

When to plant maize in northern Nigeria, according to IITA: plant as soon as the rains are established, best right after a good rain. The start of the rains changes from place to place and from year to year, so check with your ADP extension office or NiMet's seasonal forecast. IITA gives these usual best planting times:
1. Sudan savanna (the drier far north): first to second week of July.
2. Northern Guinea savanna: last week of June to first week of July.
3. Southern Guinea savanna: mid-May to the second week of June.

Land preparation for maize in the northern savannas, according to IITA: in the Guinea savannas, clear the land well before the rains and make ridges as soon as the rains start. Sow maize on well-prepared ridges 75 cm apart.

Choosing maize seed, according to IITA: use the variety recommended for your area, or ask your ADP extension agent. Buy certified seed from trusted sources, such as seed companies or community seed groups. For the dry, short-season Sudan savanna, IITA advises extra-early and early varieties.

Dressing maize seed before planting, according to IITA: treat seed with an approved seed-dressing chemical to protect it from soil pests, diseases and birds. IITA says a 10 g packet may treat 2 to 5 kg of seed, depending on the product. Put the seed and powder in a closed container and shake for about 3 minutes until the seed is coated. Wash your hands with soap and water after dressing and after planting. Follow the label and wear protective clothing.

Spacing and seed rate for maize in the Nigerian savannas, according to IITA: plant at 75 cm between rows and 50 cm between stands, with 2 plants per stand. About 15 to 20 kg of seed plants one hectare (about 2½ acres). Put 3 seeds in each hole, then thin to 2 plants about 1 to 2 weeks after the seeds come up. If you intercrop maize with another crop, use wider spacing than for maize alone.

Fertiliser for maize in the northern savannas, according to IITA: first thin the plants and weed the farm, then apply fertiliser in two doses.
1. First dose: at planting or within the first week after planting, about 6 bags of NPK 15-15-15 per hectare. IITA warns that applying this dose later than one week after planting will greatly reduce the yield of early and extra-early varieties.
2. Second dose: about 2 bags of urea per hectare, 4 to 5 weeks after planting in the Southern and Northern Guinea savanna, or 3 to 4 weeks after planting for extra-early varieties in the Sudan savanna. Do the second weeding before this dose.
3. For small farms, IITA describes putting 2 bottle caps (Coca-Cola caps) filled with NPK one week after planting, and 1 cap of urea at 4 weeks after planting, into a hole made with a stick about 5 to 8 cm from the maize plant.
4. Always cover the fertiliser with soil. Do not leave it on top of the ground.

Weeding maize, according to IITA: weed twice by hand, at 2 weeks and at 4 to 5 weeks after planting. Where spear grass is a problem, you may need to weed more than twice. Do not damage the shallow maize roots. To fight Striga, combine several methods: rotate maize with soybean, plant tolerant or resistant varieties, and apply the recommended fertiliser.

Herbicides in maize, according to IITA: IITA names glyphosate before land preparation and atrazine right after planting. Do not use atrazine if you will intercrop the maize with cowpea, groundnut or soybean. Follow the label and wear protective clothing.

Fall armyworm in maize, according to IITA: this is the most important maize pest and is found in all states of Nigeria. IITA advises controlling it within the first 3 weeks after planting.

Harvesting maize, according to IITA: for fresh (green) maize, harvest when the silk has turned brown, about 50 to 70 days after planting. For grain, harvest as soon as the grain is dry enough, about 80 to 110 days after planting, depending on the variety.

Storing maize, according to IITA: dry maize to 10 to 12% moisture and keep the store very clean. The main storage pests are grain weevils. For storage without chemicals, IITA advises airtight triple-layer storage bags. IITA also describes fumigant tablets, whose fumes are poisonous to people and animals; use them only with an extension officer's guidance, follow the label and wear protective clothing.
