About this rice guide: this advice comes from the NAERLS extension bulletin "Rice Production, Processing, Utilization and Marketing in Nigeria". Some advice differs between the forest zone, the savanna, upland rice and lowland (swamp) rice. For local advice, ask your state ADP extension office.

Choosing land for rice, according to NAERLS: heavy soils in river valleys and fadama, which hold water well, are best. Choose level land where rice has been grown before. If you plant rice on the same land for more than two years, grow legumes after rice and plough rice residues into the soil.

Preparing land for rice, according to NAERLS: harrow the land twice. Ridges are not needed unless the land slopes a lot. Build bunds to hold rain water, level the land, and divide the field into plots of 50 to 100 square metres for better water control.

Choosing rice varieties, according to NAERLS: choose a variety that suits your land and market; ask your extension worker, ADP office or the National Agricultural Seed Council. For upland rice the bulletin lists FARO 55, 56, 58 and 59 (NERICA), among others; for lowland rice, FARO 44, 52, 60 and 61, among others.

Soaking rice seed before sowing, according to NAERLS: soak seeds in cold water for 12 hours or overnight, then drain. For direct seeding, air-dry them for about two hours, then sow. NAERLS also advises an insecticide and fungicide seed dressing; follow the label and wear protective clothing.

When to sow rice, according to NAERLS: sow when the rains are established.
1. Forest area: mid-March to mid-April, after 2 to 3 good rains.
2. Savanna: mid-May to mid-June, depending on the rains.

Direct seeding of rice, according to NAERLS: if more than 80% of the seed germinates, use 50 to 60 kg of seed per hectare for dibbling, 80 kg for drilling, and 80 to 100 kg for broadcasting. Plant 2 to 4 cm deep, never deeper than 5 cm. For dibbling, put 5 to 6 seeds per hole, 25 cm between rows and 20 cm within the row, and thin to 3 or 4 seedlings 2 to 3 weeks after sowing. For drilling, rows should be 15 to 20 cm apart.

Nursery and transplanting rice, according to NAERLS:
1. A nursery of 500 square metres gives enough seedlings for 1 hectare. Make raised beds about 1.2 m wide.
2. Transplant seedlings after 21 days, uprooting them carefully.
3. Plant 2 to 3 seedlings per hill, 20 cm between rows and 15 to 20 cm between plants.

Fertiliser for rice, according to NAERLS: it is best to base fertiliser on a soil test. The recommended nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium (N-P-K) in kg per hectare is 70-50-40 for upland rice in the Sahel and Northern Guinea savanna, 60-30-30 in the Southern Guinea savanna, and 80-30-30 in the forest zone and shallow swamp rice.
1. Upland rice in the Sahel, Sudan and Northern Guinea savanna: apply half the nitrogen and all the phosphorus and potassium in furrows between rows 2 to 3 weeks after planting, and the rest of the nitrogen at 6 weeks.
2. Upland rice in the Southern Guinea and forest zones: apply all N, P and K 2 to 3 weeks after planting at first weeding, and urea 6 to 7 weeks after sowing.
3. Lowland rice: apply half the nitrogen and all the phosphorus and potassium at planting, and the rest 6 to 10 weeks after planting or at panicle initiation.
4. Apply after enough rain, about 10 cm from the plant, and bury the fertiliser.

Water for rice, according to NAERLS: lower the water to about 3 cm for 4 to 5 days before applying fertiliser. Drain the field completely 10 to 15 days before harvest so the grain ripens evenly.

Weeding rice, according to NAERLS: weed first within 2 to 3 weeks after the rice comes up, then again at 5 to 6 weeks, and a third time if needed. Ask your extension agent before using herbicides, and follow the label and wear protective clothing.

Pests in rice, according to NAERLS: watch young rice for "dead hearts" caused by stem borers. NAERLS names cypermethrin or lambda-cyhalothrin for stem borer and armyworm; follow the label and wear protective clothing. Scare birds for two weeks after planting and from heading to harvest. Use traps for rats.

Harvesting rice, according to NAERLS: harvest when 80 to 85% of the grains have turned straw-coloured, about 30 to 45 days after flowering. Cut the stems 15 to 20 cm above the ground. Do not thresh on bare ground.

Drying and storing rice, according to NAERLS: dry paddy slowly for 2 to 3 days on mats to 12 to 14% moisture. Store it in a cool, dry, rat-proof store that has been cleaned first.
