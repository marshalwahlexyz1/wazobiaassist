About this weather forecast: this is from the Nigerian Meteorological Agency (NiMet) 2026 Seasonal Climate Prediction, published in February 2026, and NiMet's update for August to October 2026, published in August 2026. These are forecasts, not records of what actually happened. By October 2026 the rainy season is ending in much of the country, so this file is mostly about the end of the 2026 season. For the latest forecast for your area, check NiMet's newest updates and ask your state ADP extension office.

What NiMet predicted for 2026 overall: in February 2026, NiMet forecast an early to normal start of the rains, a normal to late end of the rains, normal to above-normal rainfall and length of season, and above-normal temperatures for most parts of the country.

When the 2026 rains are expected to end, according to NiMet's February 2026 forecast:
1. The northernmost parts of Sokoto, Katsina, Jigawa and Yobe states: around the first week of October.
2. Most other northern states: between 14 and 30 October.
3. Central states and inland southern states: from 7 November to 1 December.
4. Coastal states such as Lagos, Delta, Bayelsa, Rivers and Akwa Ibom, and parts of Ondo, Edo and Abia states: from 9 to 17 December.

States where the rains may end later or earlier than usual in 2026, according to NiMet's February 2026 forecast: most of the country was expected to have a normal end of the rains. Parts of Lagos, Ogun, Anambra, Enugu, Cross River, Benue, Nasarawa and Kaduna states were expected to have a late end, meaning the rains last longer than usual. Parts of Ogun, Osun, Ondo, Imo, Rivers, Akwa Ibom, Kogi and Niger states were expected to have an early end, with rains stopping sooner than usual.

Length of the 2026 rainy season, according to NiMet's February 2026 forecast: the season was expected to be normal in most places. A shorter season than normal was expected in parts of Borno, Yobe and Niger states. A longer season than normal was expected in Lagos, Benue and Enugu, and in parts of Ebonyi, Ogun, Oyo, Nasarawa, Anambra, Kwara, Kebbi, Kaduna, Gombe and Taraba states.

NiMet's August 2026 update on El Niño: in August 2026, NiMet reported that a strong El Niño event had started in 2026 and was getting stronger, expected to peak in November. NiMet said its effect on rainfall in Nigeria may be significant, especially on when the rains end and how long the season lasts, particularly in northern Nigeria. This means the February dates for the end of the rains may change, so farmers should follow NiMet's latest updates.

Rainfall forecast for August to October 2026, according to NiMet's August 2026 update:
1. Wetter than normal across the north, especially Sokoto, Kebbi, Zamfara, Katsina, Kano, Yobe, Borno, Bauchi and Jigawa states.
2. Drier than normal over southern Nigeria, especially Oyo, Ogun, Lagos, Osun, Ekiti, Edo and Delta states, and likely also Kogi and parts of Benue State.
3. Normal to below-normal rainfall in the south-west and Cross River State, and normal rainfall in the rest of the country.
4. Normal to above-average temperatures across Nigeria.

Advice for farmers from NiMet's August 2026 update:
1. In Borno and Yobe states, normal to below-normal August rainfall may increase the risk of lower crop yields, especially during flowering and grain filling.
2. Avoid unnecessary fertiliser application during dry spells, to reduce stress on crops. Apply fertiliser after good (effective) rainfall so the crop can take it up.
3. Watch your fields for more weeds, pests and diseases where conditions are wetter.
4. Keep drainage channels clear to reduce flood damage where above-normal rainfall is expected (Jigawa, Kano, Katsina, Zamfara, Kebbi and Sokoto states).
5. Work closely with agricultural extension officers for advice on managing your farm.

Planning for the minor season, according to NiMet's August 2026 update: because of the strong El Niño, the measures NiMet suggests include promoting drought-resistant and early-maturing crop varieties for the minor season, water conservation, and more irrigation and rainwater harvesting. Ask your ADP extension office which varieties suit your area.
