Danger signs in a mother after giving birth, from the World Health Organization (WHO) guide for health workers on pregnancy, childbirth, postpartum and newborn care: a woman who has just given birth should go to a hospital or health centre immediately, day or night, without waiting, if she has any of these signs. Bleeding from the vagina where more than 2 or 3 pads are soaked in 20 to 30 minutes after delivery, or bleeding that increases instead of decreasing after delivery. Convulsions (fits). Fast or difficult breathing. Fever and being too weak to get out of bed. Severe pain in the belly (abdomen). Pain, redness or swelling in the calf of the leg, shortness of breath, or chest pain.

Other warning signs in a mother after giving birth, from the same WHO guide: she should go to the health centre as soon as possible if she has fever, pain in the belly, feels ill, has swollen, red or tender breasts or a sore nipple, has urine dribbling or pain when passing urine, has pain in the area between the vagina and the anus or pus coming from there, has bad-smelling discharge from the vagina after the birth, or has severe depression or thoughts or attempts of suicide.

If a mother has any danger sign after giving birth, she should not wait at home. Her family should take her to the nearest health facility or primary health centre (PHC) now, day or night. If she has heavy bleeding, fits, difficult breathing, fever with weakness, or severe belly pain, she should go to the hospital immediately.

Why bleeding and infection after birth are so dangerous, from WHO: the major problems that cause around 75% of all deaths of mothers include severe bleeding, mostly bleeding after childbirth; infections, usually after childbirth; and high blood pressure during pregnancy (pre-eclampsia and eclampsia). WHO says severe bleeding after birth can kill a healthy woman within hours if no one is caring for her. WHO also says infection after childbirth can be eliminated if good hygiene is practised and if early signs of infection are recognised and treated in time. WHO says it is very important that all births are attended by skilled health professionals.

Someone should stay with the new mother, from WHO: health workers are told to advise that someone should always be near the woman for at least 24 hours after delivery, so they can respond to any change in her condition. The woman, her partner and family should plan where to go if danger signs appear, how to reach the hospital, the costs involved, and family and community support.

Check-ups for the mother after birth, from WHO's guide for health workers: the routine postnatal contacts for the mother are within 24 hours after childbirth, on day 3 (48 to 72 hours after birth), between day 7 and day 14 after birth, and a final clinic visit at 6 weeks after birth. WHO advises the woman to bring her partner or a family member to at least one visit, and to bring her home-based maternal record to the health centre, even for an emergency visit.

Staying in the facility after birth, from WHO: WHO says few women and newborns stay in the health facility for the recommended 24 hours after birth, even though this is the most important time when problems can appear.
