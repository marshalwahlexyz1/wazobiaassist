Nigeria's routine immunisation schedule comes from the National Primary Health Care Development Agency (NPHCDA). UNICEF Nigeria shares the NPHCDA schedule card so parents can know when a child needs the next vaccine. The card gives the minimum target age of the child for each vaccine, which means the youngest age at which the vaccine is due. NPHCDA says vaccines are safe, effective and paid for by government.

At birth, the NPHCDA routine immunisation schedule says a baby should receive three vaccines: BCG, OPV 0 (oral polio vaccine, given as drops in the mouth) and the hepatitis B birth dose (Hep B 0).

At 6 weeks of age, the NPHCDA routine immunisation schedule says a baby should receive: Pentavalent 1 (DPT, Hep B and Hib), Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine 1 (PCV 1), OPV 1 (oral polio drops), Rota 1 (rotavirus drops in the mouth) and IPV 1 (polio vaccine given by injection).

At 10 weeks of age, the NPHCDA routine immunisation schedule says a baby should receive: Pentavalent 2 (DPT, Hep B and Hib), Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine 2 (PCV 2), OPV 2 (oral polio drops) and Rota 2 (rotavirus drops in the mouth).

At 14 weeks of age, the NPHCDA routine immunisation schedule says a baby should receive: Pentavalent 3 (DPT, Hep B and Hib), Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine 3 (PCV 3), OPV 3 (oral polio drops), Rota 3 (rotavirus drops in the mouth) and IPV 2 (polio vaccine given by injection).

Vitamin A on the NPHCDA schedule: the NPHCDA schedule card lists the first dose of vitamin A at 6 months of age and the second dose of vitamin A at 12 months of age. Vitamin A is given by mouth.

At 9 months of age, the NPHCDA routine immunisation schedule says a child should receive: the first dose of measles vaccine, the yellow fever vaccine and the meningitis vaccine.

At 15 months of age, the NPHCDA routine immunisation schedule says a child should receive the second dose of measles vaccine (MCV 2).

HPV vaccine in Nigeria: the NPHCDA schedule card lists the HPV vaccine for children aged 9 to 13 years. At the national launch of the HPV vaccine in October 2023, NPHCDA said that every eligible girl between the ages of 9 and 14 should receive the HPV vaccine, which protects against the virus that causes most cervical cancer. Because these two NPHCDA sources give different age ranges, parents should ask at the nearest primary health centre (PHC) which girls can get the HPV vaccine now and how many doses are needed.

If a child has missed any vaccine on the schedule, or the parent is not sure which vaccines the child has received, take the child to the nearest primary health centre (PHC) or health worker. The health worker can check what the child still needs.
