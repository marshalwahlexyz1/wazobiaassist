# Health sources: review notes

Date accessed for all sources: 2026-10-08. Every fact in the files below comes from a page or PDF that was opened in full on that date (web search summaries were used only to find pages, never as a source).

"WHO PCPNC" below means: WHO, UNFPA, UNICEF, World Bank. *Pregnancy, childbirth, postpartum and newborn care: a guide for essential practice*, 3rd edition, 2015 (ISBN 978 92 4 154935 6). Opened as a PDF from https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/e9f751fd-6eab-42cb-9e0c-0eb86a0365bb/download (catalogue page: https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249580). It is a guide for health workers; all wording was turned into plain English.

General points for the health reviewer:
- Each symptom file has a "go to the nearest health facility or primary health centre (PHC) now" paragraph. That referral wording was required by the WazobiaAssist rules and is not a quote. Its lists of signs repeat the signs given in the WHO text in the same file.
- No drug doses are given anywhere. Drug names were left out except ORS and zinc (diarrhoea), which the brief asked for.
- Plain-English substitutions to check: "belly" for abdomen, "fits" for convulsions, "poo" for stool.

## immunisation-schedule.md
- URLs:
  - https://www.unicef.org/nigeria/documents/nigeria-immunization-schedule (UNICEF Nigeria page "Nigeria Immunization Schedule", which links the PDF)
  - https://www.unicef.org/nigeria/media/9911/file/Nigeria%20Immunization%20Schedule.pdf.pdf (one-page schedule card with the Nigerian coat of arms and the NPHCDA logo; PDF metadata created 2024-07-29). The PDF is an image with no text layer, so it was rendered and read visually.
  - https://nphcda.gov.ng/speech-by-the-executive-director-ceo-of-national-primary-health-care-development-agency-nphcda-at-the-national-flag-off-of-human-papilloma-virus-vaccine-introduction-hpv-vi-on-the/ (HPV launch speech: girls aged 9-14; rollout from 24 October 2023)
  - https://nphcda.gov.ng/protecting-the-future-nigeria-launches-integrated-campaign-against-measles-rubella-human-papillomavirus-hpv-polio-and-neglected-tropical-diseases/ ("Vaccines are safe, effective and paid for by government")
- To double-check:
  - Confirm this card is the CURRENT NPHCDA schedule. It is dated 2024. NPHCDA's measles-rubella page (https://nphcda.gov.ng/measles-rubella-vaccine/) describes a measles-rubella (MR) campaign; if MR vaccine has since replaced the measles-only doses at 9 and 15 months in routine immunisation, the file must be updated. Malaria vaccine is not on the card; check whether it is now part of routine immunisation in any states.
  - HPV conflict: the card says "9-13 years, HPV 6 months interval (2 doses)". The NPHCDA launch speech says girls aged 9-14, and the Nigeria Immunization Technical Advisory Group (NGI-TAG) dossier (https://www.nitag-resource.org/sites/default/files/2023-02/Updated%20HPV%20recommendation%20on%20i%20dose%20regimen%20Technical%20dossier.pdf, opened but not used as a source) discusses moving from 2 doses to 1 dose for ages 9-14. The file states both age ranges, tells parents to ask at the PHC, and leaves out the number of doses on purpose. Please give the current official rule.
  - The card marks OPV with "*", Hep B 0 with "**", and Rota and HPV with "***", but the footnotes are not shown on the card. Ask NPHCDA what they mean.
  - Short explanations were added to some names on the card: OPV = "oral polio vaccine", IPV = "polio vaccine given by injection", Rota = "rotavirus drops". These come from the route column on the card ("Oral"/"Intramuscular"), not from a written definition. Please confirm.
  - The card also lists dosage, route and injection site for each vaccine and vitamin A amounts; these were left out on purpose (no doses).
  - The last paragraph ("If a child has missed any vaccine ... the health worker can check") is WazobiaAssist referral wording, not a quote.
  - WARNING about nphcda.gov.ng: on 2026-10-08 its home page and the /resources/publications/ and /resources/iec-materials/ pages contained many injected links to gambling sites, which suggests the website has been compromised. The two NPHCDA articles used looked normal, but NPHCDA should be told and a printed NPHCDA card or an NPHCDA officer should confirm the schedule. No schedule document could be found on nphcda.gov.ng itself; https://nphcda.gov.ng/2025/04/25/hpv-vaccine/ returned "nothing was found".

## newborn-danger-signs.md
- URLs: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/newborn-mortality ; WHO PCPNC (section K14 "Advise when to return with the baby", PDF page 155; section K2 on breastfeeding)
- To double-check:
  - The two WHO lists overlap but are not the same: the fact sheet lists feeding problems, reduced activity, difficult breathing, fever, fits, feels cold; PCPNC adds bleeding, diarrhoea, "very small, just born" and "not feeding at all", plus a second list for the health centre (pus from eyes, skin pustules, yellow skin, red or pus-draining cord, feeds fewer than 5 times in 24 hours). Both are given, each credited.
  - PCPNC is from 2015. Check it against the WHO 2022 postnatal care recommendations and Nigeria's own newborn care guidance (FMoH / NPHCDA).
  - Visit timings for the baby were left out on purpose: PCPNC K14 (first visit at 3 days) and D28 (first contact within 24 hours) differ.

## mother-after-delivery.md
- URLs: WHO PCPNC (section D28 "Advise on when to return", PDF page 64); https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/maternal-mortality ; https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/newborn-mortality (24-hour stay)
- To double-check:
  - Bleeding threshold "more than 2 or 3 pads soaked in 20 to 30 minutes" is quoted from PCPNC D28 (counselling for the woman). Elsewhere PCPNC uses "pad soaked in less than 5 minutes" for health-worker assessment of heavy bleeding; that was not used. Confirm the counselling version is the one to give the public.
  - "Pain in the area between the vagina and the anus" is a plain-English version of "pain in the perineum"; "bad-smelling discharge" is "foul-smelling lochia". Please check.
  - The file mentions severe depression and suicidal thoughts as a reason to go to the health centre (from PCPNC). Consider whether WazobiaAssist needs a Nigerian mental-health helpline added from an official source.
  - Postnatal contacts (within 24 hours, day 3, day 7-14, 6 weeks) are from PCPNC 2015; confirm against current Nigerian FMoH postnatal policy.

## malaria-and-fever.md
- URLs: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/malaria ; WHO PCPNC (K14 newborn fever; C15 pregnant woman with fever)
- To double-check:
  - The WHO fact sheet gives both 75% and 76% for the share of malaria deaths in under-5s in the African Region; the file says "about three quarters".
  - "Nigeria had the largest share of malaria deaths in the African Region in 2024" comes from the fact sheet line "Nigeria (31.9%)". Confirm the year matches the World malaria report 2025.
  - Drug names on the fact sheet (artemisinin-based combination therapy, chloroquine, primaquine) and preventive medicines (SMC, PMC, IPTp, chemoprophylaxis) were left out on purpose. A Nigerian National Malaria Elimination Programme (NMEP) source would be needed before adding anything on SMC or IPTp.
  - Malaria vaccine was left out (rollout in Nigeria is not on the NPHCDA card).
  - The fact sheet is about malaria; it does not cover other causes of fever in children. The referral paragraph tells people to go to the PHC for fever with danger signs.

## diarrhoea-in-children.md
- URLs: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/diarrhoeal-disease ; WHO PCPNC (K14 newborn with diarrhoea)
- To double-check:
  - The fact sheet says a "10-14 day" course of zinc; the length was left out as dosing information. The fact sheet's "ORS is a mixture of clean water, salt and sugar" is kept, but no home-mix recipe is given; the file says to ask a health worker how to prepare ORS. Decide whether an official FMoH/UNICEF ORS preparation message should be added.
  - The dehydration signs are WHO's classification for health workers (two or more signs = severe dehydration). The skin-pinch test is described without saying where on the body; please check that it is understandable.

## pneumonia-in-children.md
- URLs: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/pneumonia (the old address .../pneumonia-in-children returned 404) ; WHO PCPNC (K14 newborn difficult breathing)
- To double-check:
  - The fact sheet does not give breathing-rate cut-offs for "fast breathing", so none are given. Community health workers may want an official counting method added from WHO IMCI or FMoH material.
  - The fact sheet names amoxicillin and cotrimoxazole; both were left out on purpose.
  - Death figures are for 2019 (the latest on the fact sheet).

## pregnancy-danger-signs.md
- URLs: https://github.com/WorldHealthOrganization/smart-anc/blob/master/input/l2/WHO-SRH-21.2-eng.xlsx (WHO Digital Adaptation Kit for antenatal care, Web Annex B, 2021, from WHO's public GitHub repository, commit 2ed771d) ; WHO PCPNC (section C15, PDF page 44), added on 2026-10-08 because it gives the same severe danger signs (vaginal bleeding, convulsions, severe headache with blurred vision, fever and too weak to get out of bed, severe abdominal pain, fast or difficult breathing) and part of the "other signs" list (fever, abdominal pain, feels ill, swelling of fingers, face, legs). The text of the file was not changed.
- To double-check:
  - The DAK file name says WHO-SRH-21.2 but its cover says Web Annex B; confirm the official document number.
  - The list of women advised to give birth in a facility (age 17 or under, first pregnancy, 6 or more previous births, etc.) is from WHO IMPAC via the DAK. Check against Nigerian FMoH policy, which may advise facility birth for all women.

## antenatal-care-visits.md
- URLs: https://github.com/WorldHealthOrganization/smart-anc/blob/master/input/l2/WHO-SRH-21.2-eng.xlsx ; https://github.com/WorldHealthOrganization/smart-anc/blob/master/input/l2/WHO-SRH-21.3-eng.xlsx (Web Annex C: Indicators)
- To double-check:
  - This is the WHO 2016 model with 8 contacts (12, 20, 26, 30, 34, 36, 38, 40 weeks). WHO PCPNC (2015) still uses the older 4-visit model, so it was NOT added as a source here. Confirm Nigeria's current FMoH ANC policy before presenting 8 contacts as Nigerian advice.
  - The schedule says the first contact is recommended before 20 weeks and planned for 12 weeks, while the indicator measures first contact before 12 weeks. The file states both.
  - HIV, hepatitis and TB testing depend on local disease levels; the file says "tests such as HIV and syphilis tests". Please check this wording.
  - Malaria prevention medicine in pregnancy (IPTp) was left out on purpose (no drug names or doses). An approved, dose-free line from FMoH or NMEP could be added.
