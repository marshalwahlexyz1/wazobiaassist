# Laws sources review: data protection and cybercrime (2026-10-10)

Every URL below was opened on **2026-10-10** (date accessed). Each PDF was downloaded and its text extracted with pypdf. The 2024 amendment PDF is a scan with no text layer, so its pages were rendered to images and read visually. A script then checked every quote taken from a text PDF against the extracted source text, ignoring whitespace. All quotes matched. The only gaps were where a quote runs across a page break or past a margin note.

## ndpa-2023-data-protection.md

**Law and version:** Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 37 of 2023). The copy is the Federal Republic of Nigeria Official Gazette No. 119, Vol. 110, Lagos, 1st July 2023, Government Notice No. 82 (pages A717–758). Its date of enactment is "[12th Day of June, 2023]". This is the original Act, and no amendment was found.

URLs (all official NDPC):
- https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf (linked from https://ndpc.gov.ng/resources/)
- https://ndpc.gov.ng/contact/ (address, email, phone)
- https://services.ndpc.gov.ng/breach/ (reached from "Privacy Breach Reporting" in the ndpc.gov.ng menu)

Sections covered: 2, 3, 24, 25, 26, 27, 30, 31, 34, 35, 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 46, 47, 48, 50, 51 and 65 (definitions).

What a lawyer should double-check:
- **Complaint channel.** The Act (s.46) gives the right to complain but names no channel. The contact details (address, `info@ndpc.gov.ng`, `+234 (0) 916 061 5551`) are copied from https://ndpc.gov.ng/contact/. The page prints the address over three lines, and the file joins them into one. The breach portal page shows only the heading "Report a Breach in Data Protection and Privacy Violation", and its form loads by script. It was not checked whether the portal is meant for members of the public as well as organisations. The file only describes it, using the page's own words. No complaint-specific email or form was found on an official page. Confirm the right channel for an individual's complaint.
- **General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) 2025.** NDPC links a GAID 2025 at https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf. It may set procedures and timelines, such as response times for data subject requests, portability rules and complaint handling. It was not used. The Act itself gives no number of days for answering a data subject request ("without constraint or unreasonable delay"; "without undue delay").
- **Portability (s.38).** The Act says the Commission "may make regulations establishing" the right. Check whether such regulations or the GAID now make it fully operative.
- **Children (s.31(5)).** Regulations for children aged 13 and above are left to the Commission. Check whether any were issued.
- **Penalties.** Only s.48 penalty amounts were considered, and those were later trimmed from the file. The file has no criminal penalty figures.
- **Typography.** The Gazette sets a space before ";" and ":". The quotes drop that space. In s.43(1) the PDF prints "section ż42", a stray character. That section was later removed from the file.
- **Translations.** NDPC also publishes Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba versions of the Act (Book_NDPAct_Hausa/Igbo/Yoruba.pdf on ndpc.gov.ng). They may be useful for WazobiaAssist and were not reviewed.

## cybercrimes-act.md

**Law and version:** Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc) Act, 2015 (Act No. 17, 2015), as amended by the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) (Amendment) Act, 2024.

URLs:
- 2015 Act: https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/images/Downloads/downloads/cybercrime.pdf, published by the Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU), a federal government agency.
- 2024 Amendment: https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Cybercrimes-Prohibition-Prevention-etc-Amendment-Act-2024.pdf, published by the Policy and Legal Advocacy Centre, an NGO. The document is a 7-page scan. It contains the Clerk to the National Assembly's certificate under the Acts Authentication Act, dated 27 February 2024, and the President's assent in February 2024. Passage dates shown: Senate 21 December 2023, House of Representatives 14 February 2024. **This is not a Government Gazette copy.**
- Also opened but not used as a source: https://www.judy.legal/legislation/akn/ng/act/2024/cppa. It confirms the title, the list of amended sections and "Assented to on 28 February 2024", but its full text sits behind a login. The amendment is also listed at https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/22699, but the PDF link returned an HTML page.

What a lawyer should double-check:
- **The 2015 text is not the Gazette version.** The NFIU copy is a typed text headed "A Bill For An act...". A second unofficial copy (asianlaws.org) reads the same. The official ngCERT copy (cert.gov.ng) returned HTTP 403. pypdf split some words with stray spaces (for example "d eliver" and "th an"). These were joined in the quotes, and the checking script ignores whitespace. Please compare all quotes with the Gazette.
- **Section 22 (identity theft and impersonation).** In this copy, s.22(2) and (3) have no penalty of their own. The penalty "not more than 5 years or ... N7,000,000.00" appears only at the end of s.22(4). The file gives the penalty only for s.22(4). Check the Gazette to see whether that penalty also covers s.22(2)–(3).
- **Section 24 as amended.** The 2024 amendment replaces only paragraphs (a) and (b) of s.24(1). The file quotes the 2015 opening words and penalty, then the new (a) and (b), separately. It does not quote a merged text. Confirm that the s.24(1) penalty (fine up to N7,000,000.00 and/or up to 3 years) is unchanged. In the 2015 copy, s.24(2)(ii) refers to "paragraph (c) and (d)", but s.24(2) has no paragraph (d). Only the (i) penalty for paragraphs (a) and (b) was quoted.
- **Section 17 drafting mismatch.** The amendment changes "geniuses" in "subsection (2)" and adds words in "subsection (4)". In the NFIU copy, "geniuses" is in s.17(1)(b) and there is no s.17(4). The s.17 block was removed from the file to save space, but the summary block still lists s.17 as amended.
- **Section 21.** The amendment changes the reporting deadline from "7 days of its occurance" to "72 hours of its detection". The s.21 block was removed from the final file.
- **Section 44 (cybersecurity levy) and section 41** are amended, but these are not covered because they are not about individuals' rights.
- **SIM swap.** Neither Act uses the term "SIM swap". The closing block in the file names related sections: s.22, s.33, s.36, s.37(3) and the "access device" definition in s.58. Saying these sections "may be relevant" is an editorial judgement, not legal text. Please confirm or remove it. Any CBN or NCC rules on SIM swap were not collected.
- **Section 19(3)** puts the burden of proving negligence on the customer after a security breach. Check whether later CBN consumer-protection rules affect how this is explained to users.
- **Section 37(3).** The 72-hour reversal rule for unauthorised debits is quoted exactly. Confirm it with the Gazette, since many users will rely on it.
- **Section 48(4)** (cancelling a convict's passport) was deleted by the 2024 amendment. The file says so.
- **Child offences (s.23).** As instructed, the file names the offences and the age of a child (below 18) only. No penalties and no definitions of sexual conduct are given.
- **Penalties** are quoted exactly from the NFIU copy where given. Some figures in that copy have no "N" (for example "not less than 7,000,000.00" in s.14(1)). Section 14(1) was later removed, and s.14(2) has the "N".
