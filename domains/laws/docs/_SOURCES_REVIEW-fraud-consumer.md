# Sources review: fraud and consumer files (laws domain)

Date accessed for all sources: 2026-10-10.
Every quotation in these files was checked word for word against the source text named below. Sections not relevant to ordinary people were left out. No text from commentary or summary sites appears inside "The law says" quotes.

## 1. efcc-act-2004.md

- Law and version: Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (Establishment, etc.) Act, 2004 (2004 No. 1; commencement 4 June 2004), as printed in the Laws of the Federation of Nigeria 2004, Cap. E1.
- Primary source (unofficial): Policy and Legal Advocacy Centre (PLAC), https://www.placng.org/lawsofnigeria/laws/E1.pdf
- Cross-check (unofficial): PolicyVault Africa, https://policyvault.africa/wp-content/uploads/policy/NGA1185.pdf. The two copies match word for word on every section quoted.
- Official copy: not obtained. efcc.gov.ng is a JavaScript single-page site, and no Act PDF could be downloaded from it. A copy hosted by the Nigeria Governors' Forum repository (ngfrepository.org.ng:8443) did not respond.
- Things the source copy does that a lawyer should know about:
  - Section 20(1)(a) reads "which mayor are" in both copies (a typo for "may or"). The quote leaves those words out with "...".
  - Section 29 lists paragraphs "(b)" and "(c)" and has no "(a)" in both copies. It is quoted as printed.
  - Section 7(2)(a) has marginal citations ("2003 No.7, 1995 No. 13") run into the text. They are left out with "...".
- What a lawyer should double-check:
  - Whether the 2004 Act has since been amended or replaced. As of the access date I found no evidence that a repeal or re-enactment had been signed. An amendment bill (HB. 2493) was reported in the House of Representatives, but its status is unknown.
  - Section 1(2)(c), which made the EFCC the Financial Intelligence Unit. It was left out of the file because the Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit Act 2018 is understood to have moved that role to the NFIU. Please confirm.
  - Section 7(2) names the Money Laundering Act 2004 and the Advance Fee Fraud Act 1995. Both have since been replaced: by the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 and by the Advance Fee Fraud and Other Fraud Related Offences Act 2006.
  - Forfeiture provisions (sections 20 to 34), in light of the Proceeds of Crime (Recovery and Management) Act 2022.
  - Section 15 (terrorism) was left out because of the Terrorism (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022.
  - Arrest, detention and bail procedure, which now falls under the Administration of Criminal Justice Act 2015, plus any Supreme Court rulings on the EFCC's powers.
  - The penalty amounts in sections 16, 17, 18, 27, 38 and 39, which are quoted from the unofficial copy.

## 2. advance-fee-fraud-act-2006.md (the "419" law)

- Law and version: Advance Fee Fraud and Other Fraud Related Offences Act, 2006 (commencement 5 June 2006). The PLAC copy is marked "[2007 No.62.]".
- Primary source (unofficial): PLAC Laws of Nigeria, https://placng.org/lawsofnigeria/print.php?sn=18
- Cross-checks (unofficial):
  - PolicyVault Africa, https://policyvault.africa/wp-content/uploads/policy/NGA1149.pdf. It matches PLAC word for word on every section quoted.
  - DataGuidance, https://www.dataguidance.com/sites/default/files/advance_fee_fraud_and_other_fraud_related_offences_act_2006.pdf. This copy differs in places:
    - Section 1(1) has an extra "(c) obtains any property ...".
    - Numbers are written as words ("seven years" instead of "7 years").
    - Section 19 reads "this Bill" instead of "this Act".
    - It has minor spelling differences.
  - The PLAC/PolicyVault wording was used. A lawyer should confirm section 1(1) against the official gazette.
- Official copy: not obtained.
- Section numbers: the PLAC web copy prints no number beside the headings of sections 2 to 8. The numbers come from the arrangement of sections and agree with the DataGuidance copy.
- What a lawyer should double-check:
  - Any amendments after 2006.
  - Section 18 (arrest under the Criminal Procedure Act or Criminal Procedure Code), since the Administration of Criminal Justice Act 2015 and the state ACJ laws now largely govern this.
  - How section 7 (money laundering) and section 17 (forfeiture without conviction) interact with the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 and the Proceeds of Crime (Recovery and Management) Act 2022.
  - Online scams may also fall under the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015 as amended in 2024, which is not covered here.

## 3. fccpa-2018-consumer-rights.md

- Law and version: Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act, 2018 (2019 No. 1, Federal Republic of Nigeria Official Gazette), as published by the FCCPC.
- Primary source (official): FCCPC, https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf, linked from https://fccpc.gov.ng/resources-library/fccpa/
- Contact block: FCCPC contact page, https://fccpc.gov.ng/about-us/contact/, and the complaints portal link on it (https://complaints.fccpc.gov.ng). That block is marked as website information, not law.
- Method: the official PDF is a scanned gazette with a poor OCR text layer. Every quoted passage was checked against the rendered page images of the gazette: pages A12 to A13 (s.17), A60 to A79 (ss.114 to 155) and A86 to A89 (s.167). Errors from the text layer were corrected to match the printed page.
- A section-by-section copy at lawglobalhub.com was used only to help read the scans. No text was taken from it.
- Sections covered:
  - Definitions (s.167).
  - Part XV consumer rights (ss.114 to 132, except s.126).
  - Part XVI duties of manufacturers and suppliers (ss.135, 136, 142, 143, 145).
  - Part XVII enforcement (ss.146, 148, 151, 152) and s.155 penalties.
  - Left out to keep the file within length: ss.17, 133, 147, 149, 150, 153 and 154.
- What a lawyer should double-check:
  - Any amendment to the FCCPA since 2018.
  - Any final FCCPC Consumer Protection Regulations. An exposure draft of FCCP Consumer Protection Regulations was posted on fccpc.gov.ng in April 2026 and may set the "prescribed manner and form" for complaints under s.148.
  - The Digital, Electronic, Online or Non-Traditional Consumer Lending Regulations 2025, which matter for loan-app complaints.
  - The relationship between the FCCPC and sector regulators (for example CBN, NCC and NERC) in regulated industries (Part XIII, s.104 onwards).
  - The penalty in s.155(b), quoted as printed: "not less than ₦100,000,000.00 or 10% of its turnover".
  - The FCCPC contact details, which can change.
