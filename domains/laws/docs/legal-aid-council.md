This file explains how to get free legal help from the Legal Aid Council of Nigeria (LAC or LACoN). It uses the Council's official website and the Legal Aid Act, 2011, which is the law that sets up the Council. The Police Act 2020 (section 35) and the Administration of Criminal Justice Act 2015 (section 6) say that the police must tell an arrested person of the right to free legal representation by the Legal Aid Council of Nigeria where applicable.

Legal Aid Council: what it is and what it does. The Legal Aid Council of Nigeria was set up by law to give free legal help to poor (indigent) Nigerians. Its website says its mission is "To ensure free, effective and efficient legal aid services to indigents in Nigeria". The website says the Legal Aid Act 2011 expanded the Council's mandate "to provide free legal assistance and representation, Legal advice and Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) to indigent Nigerians in conflict with the law as to enhance access to Justice in Nigeria."

Legal Aid Act 2011, section 8: Scope of legal aid and access to justice (three services). The Council gives legal aid in three broad areas: a Criminal Defence Service, advice and assistance in civil matters including representation in court, and Community Legal Services. All of these are subject to a merits test and a test of whether the person is poor (indigence).
The law says: "(1) The grant of legal aid, advice and access to justice shall be provided by the Council in 3 broad areas, namely, Criminal Defence Service, Advice and Assistance in Civil matters including legal representation in court and Community Legal Services subject to merits and indigence tests for the parties. (2) The Council, shall establish, maintain and develop a service known as the Criminal Defence Service for the purpose of assisting indigent persons involved in criminal investigation or proceedings specified in the Second Schedule to this Act, access to such advice, assistance and representation as the interest of justice requires. (3) The Council shall establish and maintain a service to be known as the Civil Litigation Service for the purpose of assisting indigent persons to access such advice, assistance, and representation in court where the interest of justice demands, to secure, defend, enforce, protect or otherwise exercise any right, obligation, duty, privilege interest or service to which that person is ordinarily entitled under the Nigerian legal system."

Legal Aid Act 2011, section 10: Persons entitled to legal aid (who qualifies). Legal aid is given only to a person whose income is not more than the national minimum wage. In exceptional circumstances, the Council's Board may still give legal aid to a person who earns more than the minimum wage. The court fee rules do not apply to a person granted legal aid. (In the scanned copy of the Act on the Council's website, this section's number is printed as "6.", but the Act's list of sections calls it section 10, "Persons entitled to legal aid".)
The law says: "(1) Legal aid shall only be granted to a person whose income does not exceed the national minimum wage. (2) Notwithstanding the provision of subsection (1), the Board may, in exceptional circumstance, grant legal aid service to a person whose earning exceeds the national minimum wage. ... (5) The rules of any court relating to payment of fees shall not apply to a person granted legal aid."

Legal Aid Act 2011, section 11: Ascertainment of means (how your income is checked). To decide whether you qualify, the Council looks at your income and your property.
The law says: "11.(1) In ascertaining the means of any person for the purposes of this Act, that person's income and his personal and real property shall be taken into account. (2) In assessing a person's means such of his commitments as may be prescribed, shall be deducted from the resources, which would otherwise be his means."

Legal Aid Council: criminal cases it handles (Criminal Code and Penal Code offences). The Council's website lists the crimes for which it gives free criminal defence. Under the Criminal Code (Southern States) these are murder, manslaughter, grievous hurt, assault occasioning actual bodily harm, common assault, affray, stealing, rape and armed robbery. Under the Penal Code (Northern States and the FCT) these are culpable homicide punishable with death, culpable homicide not punishable with death, wounding or inflicting grievous bodily harm, criminal force occasioning actual bodily harm, common assault, affray, stealing, rape and armed robbery. It also covers helping, planning or attempting any of these offences.
The Legal Aid Council website says: "The provision of legal assistance to indigent persons in the form of advice, assistance and representation in court in matters of criminal investigation or proceedings in any court or tribunal (whether at first instance or on appeal) wholly or partly in respect of crimes of the following descriptions, or as near to those descriptions as may be, respectively in any Criminal Code or Penal Code" ... "Aiding and abetting, or counseling or procuring the commission of, or being an accessory before or after the fact to, or attempting or conspiring to commit, any of the offenses listed above."

Legal Aid Council: civil cases it handles (including fundamental rights). The Council's Civil Justice Department helps poor people with civil matters. The website lists claims for accidents (including employees' compensation claims), claims for breach of fundamental rights under Chapter IV of the Constitution, and civil claims arising from crimes against people who qualify for legal aid.
The Legal Aid Council website says: "The provision of legal assistance to indigent persons in the form of advice, assistance and representation in court in order to secure, defend, enforce, protect or otherwise exercise any right, obligation, duty, privilege, interest or service to which that person is ordinarily entitled under the Nigerian Legal System;" ... "To pursue civil claims in respect of accidents including employees compensation claims (under the Employee's Compensation Act No. 13 of 2010;" ... "To pursue civil claims to cover breach of Fundamental Rights guaranteed under Chapter IV of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria;" ... "To pursue civil claims arising from criminal activities against persons who are qualified for Legal Aid under the Legal Aid Act 2011".

Legal Aid Act 2011, section 15: Demand for or receipt of certain payments prohibited. A lawyer who acts for a person receiving legal aid is paid by the Council's legal aid fund. The lawyer is not entitled to charge or recover costs for that work from the person being helped.
The law says: "(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act a legal practitioner or other legal service providers who has acted for a person receiving legal aid shall be paid for so acting by the receipt of certain legal aid Fund and the legal practitioner or legal service provider shall not be entitled to payments charge or recover from a legally assisted person any amount- (a) by way of costs in respect of work assigned by the Council to a private legal practitioner on behalf of that person; or (b) by way of disbursement incurred on behalf of that person in connection with that work except with the approval of the Governing Council."

Legal Aid Act 2011, section 19: Police and courts must tell a suspect about legal aid; Council can visit police cells. Police officers and courts must tell a suspected person, from the moment of arrest, that he is entitled to a lawyer. If the suspect cannot afford a lawyer, they must notify the Council to represent him if he wants. The Council inspects prisons and police cells, and its lawyers can interview suspects and be present during their interrogation.
The law says: "(1) The Council shall, from time to time, conduct inspection of prisons, police cells and other places where suspected persons are held in order to assess the circumstances under which such persons are detained. (2) It shall be the duty of all police officers and courts to inform suspected person of his entitlement to the services of a legal practitioner from the moment of arrest and if such suspect cannot afford the services of a legal practitioner to notify the Council to represent him if he so desires. (3) The Council and the lawyers designated by it shall be entitled to have access to and interview suspects detained in prisons, police stations, or any other places of detention in Nigeria and such designated lawyers shall be entitled to be present during the interrogation of the suspects in accordance with the rights guaranteed to suspects under the Constitution. ... (5) The Council may file an application in any appropriate court for the review of the case of any person who has been held in any place of custody without trial for a period exceeding the maximum provided by the Constitution."

Legal Aid Act 2011, section 21: Penalty for false information. A person who asks for legal aid must tell the truth. Knowingly or recklessly giving false information is an offence.
The law says: "If a person seeking or receiving legal aid or advice in furnishing any information required under or pursuant to this Act, knowingly or recklessly makes any statement which is false in a material particular, he commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of N50,000.00 or imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months or to both such fine and imprisonment."
