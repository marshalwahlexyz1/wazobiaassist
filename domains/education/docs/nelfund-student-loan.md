The Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND) gives zero-interest loans to Nigerian students for higher education. It was set up under the Students Loans (Access to Higher Education) Act, 2024. The loan pays institutional charges (school fees) and, if the student asks for it, a monthly upkeep allowance. School fees are paid directly to the school, and upkeep is paid to the student's bank account.

Who can apply, according to NELFUND:
1. You must be a Nigerian citizen, shown by your National Identification Number (NIN) and Bank Verification Number (BVN). The guidelines say the BVN is required for applicants aged 18 and above.
2. You must have admission into a public university, polytechnic, college of education or vocational school in Nigeria. NELFUND's FAQ says the loan is open to all public tertiary institutions, but that the first phase was only for students in federal institutions. Check nelf.gov.ng for the current list of eligible schools.
3. You need a JAMB number. New students, existing students and Direct Entry students can apply. There is no age limit.

You cannot get the loan if you have defaulted on a previous education loan from government, already have a government scholarship or education loan, were dismissed for exam malpractice or fake documents, or were convicted of fraud, forgery, drug offences, cultism, felony or other offences involving dishonesty. The guidelines also say you must not already hold a first degree.

How to apply, according to nelf.gov.ng:
1. Go to nelf.gov.ng and click "APPLY NOW" to register on the student loan portal (https://portal.nelf.gov.ng/auth/login).
2. Fill in your details: name of your institution, admission number, JAMB number, date of birth, NIN and BVN.
3. Upload your documents. The FAQ says a scanned admission letter is compulsory for new students, and a student ID card is optional.
4. Click "Request for Student Loan", complete the application steps and submit.
5. If you want the monthly upkeep, apply for both the institutional loan and the upkeep loan when you apply. NELFUND says students who apply only for the institutional loan will not get the upkeep allowance.
6. Your application is reviewed. You get a notification, and you can see your status in your profile on the portal.

NELFUND says you do not pay anything before the loan is paid out. You do not need a guarantor. NELFUND says it pays out within 30 days of approving an application. You must apply again every academic session.

Repaying the loan, according to NELFUND:
1. Repayment starts 2 years after you finish NYSC.
2. If you are employed, 10% of your monthly salary is deducted by your employer.
3. If you are self-employed, you pay 10% of your monthly profit to the Fund.
4. If you have no job two years after NYSC, you must notify NELFUND with a sworn court affidavit every three months.
5. You can pay back early, in full or in part, at any time.

By accepting the loan terms, the student agrees that if he or she fails to repay when due, NELFUND can recover the money from any bank account linked to the student, through a Global Standing Instruction. Deliberate default can lead to penalties, legal action and damage to your credit score. NELFUND may forgive the loan in case of death, and may write it off for severe or permanent disability.

If your application is denied, you can raise a complaint on the portal or email NELFUND. The NELFUND website's "Get in touch" link goes to https://nelfund.esupport.ng/.
