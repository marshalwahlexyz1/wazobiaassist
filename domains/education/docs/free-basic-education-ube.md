In Nigeria, basic education in public schools is free and compulsory. This comes from the Compulsory, Free Universal Basic Education Act, 2004 (the UBE Act). The Act says every government in Nigeria shall provide free, compulsory and universal basic education for every child of primary and junior secondary school age.

The UBE Act defines basic education as early childhood care and education plus nine years of formal schooling. UBEC says the nine years are six years of primary school and three years of junior secondary school (JSS). A "child or ward" under the Act is a person of primary or junior secondary school age, between 6 and 16 years old, whether disabled or not.

Free means free. Section 3 of the Act says the services provided in public primary and junior secondary schools shall be free of charge. Anyone who collects a fee against this rule commits an offence. The Act, as written in 2004, sets the punishment as a fine of not more than N10,000, or 3 months in prison, or both.

Parents have a duty too. The Act says every parent shall make sure that his or her child or ward attends and completes primary school and junior secondary school. Every parent must also make sure the child gets full-time education suitable to the child's age, ability and aptitude, by attending school regularly.

A parent who does not send a child to school commits an offence under the Act. As written in 2004, the punishment is:
1. First conviction: a reprimand (a formal warning).
2. Second conviction: a fine of N2,000, or 1 month in prison, or both.
3. Further convictions: a fine of N5,000, or 2 months in prison, or both.

The Act says a Magistrate Court or other State court can hear these cases. Stakeholders in education in each Local Government Area must make sure parents carry out this duty. These duties do not apply to a parent or child living outside Nigeria.

Who runs basic education. The Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC) is the Federal Government agency that coordinates the UBE programme. Under the Act, the Federal Government only assists the States and Local Governments so that basic education is uniform and of good quality across Nigeria. Each State has a State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB), and each Local Government Area and FCT Area Council has a Local Government Education Authority. The UBEC website links to the SUBEB of many states.

UBEC also says that moving from primary school to junior secondary school should be automatic, because basic education ends at JSS. It says an entrance examination may no longer be necessary, and that final examination and certification happen at the end of the nine years of basic education.
