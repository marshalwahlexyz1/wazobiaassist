WAEC (the West African Examinations Council) conducts the WASSCE (West African Senior School Certificate Examination). WAEC Nigeria says it holds three WASSCE exams each year: one in February for private candidates, one in May/June for school candidates, and one in November/December for private candidates. Registration for both school and private candidates is done online. For school candidates, the school does all the registration.

Private candidates register on their own. According to the WAEC Nigeria FAQ:
1. Buy the entry scratch card (PIN) by paying the fee to an accredited selling agent. You get two PINs: one for online registration and one for checking your result online.
2. Fill the entry form on WAEC's registration website. The WAEC Nigeria home page links "WASSCE for Private Candidates" to https://registration.waecdirect.org/, which tells candidates to register through the WAECKONNECT app (Android or Apple stores) or on the WAECKONNECT website.
3. Print your identification photo card from the website. It shows your centre name, location, number and the subjects you entered for.

The WASSCE entry rules on the WAEC Nigeria FAQ say candidates must sit a minimum of 8 and a maximum of 9 subjects. Candidates registering for WASSCE in 2026 and 2027 sit two core subjects, English Language and General Mathematics, plus six or seven other subjects.

For the WASSCE for Private Candidates, 2026 Second Series, the WAECDIRECT page says normal registration ended on Friday, 18 September 2026. Walk-in registration then began, and candidates can buy the registration PIN at any WAEC office near them up till the end of the examination. The final timetable shows papers from Friday, 4 September 2026 to Wednesday, 28 October 2026. Always confirm dates and fees on waecnigeria.org.

How to check your WAEC result online, according to WAECDIRECT (https://www.waecdirect.org/):
1. Buy a result checker e-PIN. WAEC says e-PINs are sold at the WAEC National office, its Zonal and Branch offices across Nigeria, and designated e-PIN Mega Dealer outlets.
2. Go to https://www.waecdirect.org/.
3. Enter your 10-digit WAEC examination number. This is your 7-digit centre number followed by your 3-digit candidate number. For exam years before 1999, use your 8-digit number.
4. Enter the 4 digits of your examination year, for example 2002.
5. Choose the type of examination: private candidate results or school candidate results.
6. Enter the e-PIN voucher (serial) number and the PIN on your e-PIN.
7. Click Submit and wait for your result to show.

One e-PIN can check one result only, up to a maximum of 5 times. After that, you need a new result checker card. If you have a problem checking your result, WAECDIRECT asks you to email your examination number, exam year, exam type and the error message to customersupport@vatebra.com.

WAEC does not replace lost certificates. It issues a statement of result, or an attestation of result for damaged or lost certificates, and can confirm results for a fee. Certificates are collected from the WAEC Zonal and Branch offices in charge of the state where you sat the exam.

To correct an error on a certificate, a private candidate writes to WAEC directly, while for a school candidate the principal makes the request. The original certificate is sent with the application. WAEC charges a fee only if the error came from the candidate.

Only school candidates can ask for a review of their result, through their principal, within sixty days of the exam, and a fee is paid for each paper. WAEC says no such provision exists for private candidates.

Results are cancelled when candidates are found guilty of exam malpractice. Candidates whose results were withheld can lodge a complaint on WAEC's complaint portal, https://waecinternational.org/complaints, as stated on the WAEC Nigeria news page.
