To register for the UTME or Direct Entry (DE), you first need a JAMB profile code. Your National Identification Number (NIN) is used to create it. Fees and dates change every year, so always confirm them on the official JAMB website, jamb.gov.ng.

How to get your JAMB profile code, according to the JAMB FAQ:
1. Use a phone number that belongs only to you. JAMB says no two candidates can use the same number, not even brothers and sisters. This SIM is your link to JAMB for all future services, so do not lose it.
2. Send an SMS with the word NIN, then a space, then your 11-digit NIN, to 55019 or 66019. Example: NIN 00123456789. There must be a space between NIN and the number.
3. Your profile code is sent to your phone.

How to complete UTME or DE registration, according to the JAMB FAQ:
1. Take your profile code to a seller of the e-PIN (banks, mobile money operators, microfinance banks, switches or USSD partners) and pay the fee for the application you want. Registration is cashless.
2. Your e-PIN is sent by text message to the phone number you registered with JAMB.
3. Go in person to a JAMB-accredited CBT centre with your profile code and e-PIN. Registration can only be done at JAMB-approved CBT centres, not at any cybercafe.
4. At the centre, give your choice of institutions and courses, a working personal email address, your O'level grades or results (if you are not awaiting results), your UTME subjects and your choice of examination town. Your photograph and all ten fingerprints are captured.
5. Check that all your details are correct before you confirm with your fingerprint. Your registration slip is printed after the fingerprint check. The details on your NIN should match your other documents.

Do not register more than once. If there is an error, ask JAMB for a correction instead of buying a new application document.

Fees for the 2025 UTME, as listed in the JAMB FAQ: UTME with mock N8,700; UTME without mock N7,200; Direct Entry N5,700; trial-testing mock examination for underage candidates N3,500.

Fees for the 2026 UTME: in a statement reported by BusinessDay, JAMB said its fees were unchanged: Direct Entry N5,700, UTME only (without mock) N7,200, and UTME with mock N8,700. JAMB asked candidates who paid more than these approved fees at a CBT centre to report the centre with credible evidence. For the 2026 UTME, registration at CBT centres closed on Saturday, 28 February 2026. 2027 UTME fees and dates were not yet confirmed; check jamb.gov.ng.

For the 2025 UTME, JAMB said only candidates who would be 16 years old by 30 September 2025 could apply. Younger candidates could only take the trial mock examination. The JAMB website says the 2026 Policy Meeting kept the minimum age for admission at 16 years.

Lost profile code, password or e-PIN, according to the JAMB FAQ:
1. To get back a lost profile code, send RESEND from the same phone number to 55019 or 66019.
2. To reset a lost profile password, send the word PASSWORD, a space, and your email address to 55019 or 66019 from the same number.
3. If your UTME e-PIN is lost or not received, send UTMEPIN from your registered number to 55019 or 66019.
4. If you created a profile code last year, send RESEND to 55019 or 66019 to use it again for a new registration.

If you lost your registered SIM or email, the JAMB Bulletin of 21 September 2026 says JAMB now allows a SIM swap or email change. You can do it only once, and only at JAMB CBT (PTC/PRC) centres, including state offices where available. You need the new phone number or email and a sworn court affidavit, scanned. Log into your e-Facility profile, choose "SIM Swap/eMail Change Payment" and pay the fee, then go to the centre for fingerprint checks and the update.

Keep your registration number, passwords, email and e-PINs private. JAMB says no centre is allowed to ask for or accept your password.
