The National Identification Number (NIN) is a unique 11-digit number that the National Identity Management Commission (NIMC) gives to a person after they enrol. Enrolment records your personal details, your ten fingerprints, a head-to-shoulder photo and your signature. A NIN is never given to another person, even after the owner dies.

All Nigerian citizens and legal residents, from birth (age zero) and above, can enrol for a NIN. NIMC says it is mandatory for every citizen and legal resident to enrol. The NIN is used for things like getting a passport, opening a bank account, getting a driver's licence, getting a Permanent Voter Card, paying tax and pension.

Getting a NIN is free. NIMC's fees page lists "NIN (Adults)" and "NIN (Minors)" as FREE, and the NIMC FAQs 2026 list "NIN Enrolment – Free" and "NIN Slip – Free". In a press release of December 20, 2024, NIMC said it does not charge, and does not allow its staff or Front End Partners (FEPs) to charge, anyone for NIN enrolment.

Steps to enrol for a NIN, according to the NIMC FAQs 2026:
1. Go to a NIMC Enrolment Centre or an accredited centre with a valid means of identification. You can find centres on the NIMC website at https://nimc.gov.ng/enrolment-centres.
2. Fill the NIN Enrolment form correctly.
3. Your personal details and biometrics (fingerprints and photo) are captured. Check that all the information is correct and complete.
4. Submit your supporting documents (means of identification).
5. You are given a Transaction Slip or NIN Slip.
6. Enrolment is complete.

What to bring: the NIMC FAQs 2026 say to visit the centre with a valid government ID and your BVN if you have one. For pre-enrolment, the documents listed are an affidavit, an NPC birth certificate, a Nigerian passport, a Nigerian driver's licence, or another government-issued document.

You can save time by pre-enrolling online at https://penrol.nimc.gov.ng/ using a computer or smartphone. Click "Begin New Application" and fill the form. You get a Session ID, not a slip. Take the Session ID to a NIMC enrolment centre on your appointment date for your fingerprints and photo. If you miss the appointment, click "Rebook Application".

To enrol a child, NIMC's 2026 ward enrolment FAQ lists the child's birth certificate and the parent or guardian's NIN. The parent or guardian must have a NIN first. Children under 16 are not given the National ID card. When a child who enrolled as a minor turns 16, the child must go in person to a NIMC enrolment centre for a "migration".

After enrolment you receive a Transaction Slip at once. NIMC says the NIN is usually ready in about 24 to 72 hours. You collect your NIN yourself after your fingerprints are checked. Nobody can collect it for you, and nobody can enrol on your behalf. You must be there in person.

Enrol only once. NIMC says enrolment is once in a lifetime, and enrolling a second time will be detected and is a punishable offence.

Warning about touts and fraudsters: do not pay anyone to get your NIN. NIMC warns against people posing as NIMC agents or staff who offer to "speed up" enrolment for money, and against fake social media pages asking for payment. Always use the NIMC website https://www.nimc.gov.ng for information. To report anyone asking for money for NIN enrolment, NIMC gives the phone number 07002255646, the toll-free line 08000616462 and the email nimccustomercare@nimc.gov.ng.

Keep your NIN private. NIMC advises that you should not share it on social media or print it on business cards, and only give it to relevant authorities when asked.
