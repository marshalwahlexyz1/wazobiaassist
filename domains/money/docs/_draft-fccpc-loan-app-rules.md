The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) regulates loan apps and other digital lenders in Nigeria. In 2025 it issued the Digital, Electronic, Online, or Non-Traditional Consumer Lending Regulations, 2025, often called the DEON Regulations. The FCCPC said the rules were made because of many complaints about exploitative practices, privacy violations, abusive loan recovery and harassment by digital lenders.

The FCCPC has said that some online lenders used unethical recovery methods such as public shaming, defamation, intimidation and breaches of privacy. Earlier, a joint task force of the FCCPC, NITDA and ICPC directed lenders to stop contacting people on a borrower's contact list, including by text message, and to stop using abusive or threatening language with borrowers.

Reports on the DEON Regulations say they ban harassment of borrowers, including threats, name-calling and public shaming, and ban sending defamatory messages to a borrower's contacts, friends or family. Some commentators say the rules also stop loan apps from accessing a borrower's contacts, photos or call logs, but this has not been confirmed from the text of the regulations, so check the regulations before relying on it.

Under the regulations, a lender must resolve a customer's complaint within 24 hours {{VERIFY}}, or within 48 hours {{VERIFY}} where that is not practical.

Lenders had to regularise their status by 5 January 2026 {{VERIFY}}. The FCCPC said lenders that did not do so were removed from its published register of approved digital lenders, and it told consumers to be careful with any digital lender that is not on its current list of approved operators.

The legal status of the regulations changed during 2026. In April 2026 {{VERIFY}} a Federal High Court in Lagos made interim orders that stopped the FCCPC from enforcing parts of the regulations, after a case brought by a lenders' association, WASPAN. On 20 July 2026 {{VERIFY}} the court dismissed the case and lifted those orders, and the FCCPC said the regulations were again fully operational and enforceable. WASPAN filed an appeal dated 21 July 2026 {{VERIFY}}. The outcome of the appeal was not known when this was written, so the rules could still be affected by a later court decision.

How to report a loan app that is harassing you:
1. Keep all evidence. Take screenshots of threatening messages, call logs and social media posts, and do not delete the original messages.
2. Complain to the lender first. The FCCPC says a consumer should first take the complaint to the company, and go to the FCCPC if not satisfied.
3. Report to the FCCPC. Its digital lending notice asks consumers to report unlawful or unregistered lenders, unfair interest rates or privacy violations to lenderstaskforce@fccpc.gov.ng {{VERIFY}}. You can also use the "File a Complaint" section on the FCCPC website, fccpc.gov.ng. The FCCPC's general email is contact@fccpc.gov.ng {{VERIFY}} and its FAQ lists hotlines 08056002020 and 08056003030 {{VERIFY}}.
