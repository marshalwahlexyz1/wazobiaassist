The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) regulates loan apps and other digital lenders. On 3 September 2025 it announced the Digital, Electronic, Online, or Non-Traditional Consumer Lending Regulations, 2025, called the DEON Regulations. The FCCPC said the rules came into effect on 21 July 2025. They apply to non-bank lenders that give unsecured loans through apps, websites or other digital means, including fintech companies, mobile loan apps and microfinance institutions. They do not cover deposit money banks.

The FCCPC said that between 2021 and 2023 some online lenders used public shaming, defamation, intimidation and breaches of privacy to recover loans. Its chief executive said: "No consumer should be harassed, defamed, or lured into unsustainable debt under the guise of digital lending." Earlier, in April 2022, a joint task force of the FCCPC, NITDA and ICPC told lenders to stop contacting people on borrowers' contact lists, including by text message, and to stop using abusive or threatening language with borrowers.

Under the DEON Regulations, a lender must use clear loan terms, disclose all interest rates, fees and repayment schedules, and obey the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. Automatic or pre-authorised lending is banned: you must actively ask for a loan. The application form for lenders asks them to confirm that their apps will not have access to users' call logs, contacts and photos or gallery.

A lender must show its complaint phone numbers and email on its website or app. It must resolve your complaint within 24 hours. Where that is not possible, it must tell you how long it will take, and this must not be more than 48 hours.

The FCCPC keeps a public register of approved digital lenders on its website. On 21 January 2026 it said it had removed lenders that did not complete the required regularisation process from this register, and advised consumers to be careful with any digital lender not on its current list.

LEGAL STATUS (checked 8 October 2026): In April 2026 a Federal High Court in Lagos made an interim order in a case brought by the Wireless Application Service Providers Association of Nigeria (WASPAN), and the FCCPC suspended the regulations. On 20 July 2026 the court dismissed the case and discharged the order, and the FCCPC said the regulations were again fully in force. WASPAN filed a notice of appeal dated 21 July 2026 at the Court of Appeal. We found no Court of Appeal decision, so the rules could still change.

How to report a loan app that is harassing you or treating you unfairly:
1. Keep proof of the loan and copies of the messages or documents that support your complaint.
2. Complain to the lender first. The FCCPC says you must first try to settle the complaint with the company.
3. If you are not satisfied, report to the FCCPC by email at lenderstaskforce@fccpc.gov.ng or through the complaint window on its website, fccpc.gov.ng.
4. In your complaint, state the lender's name and correct address, the amount involved, and what you want done. Attach proof of the transaction.

Making a complaint to the FCCPC is free. If you complain electronically, you should get an acknowledgement immediately; for a paper complaint, within 2 working days. The FCCPC says redress can take from 1 day to 45 days. The FCCPC does not handle criminal matters.
