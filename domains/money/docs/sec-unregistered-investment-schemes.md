On 14 May 2026 the Securities and Exchange Commission, Nigeria (SEC) published a public notice about unregistered online investment schemes. The SEC said these schemes are increasingly promoted on WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram, Facebook, TikTok and other social media and websites.

The SEC said many of these online schemes show the signs of Ponzi or prohibited investment schemes. It advised the public not to invest in any unregistered online investment platform or scheme that promises unrealistic or guaranteed returns.

The SEC also advised the public not to rely on investment advice shared online by people or companies that are not registered with the SEC, because this can lead to big financial losses and fraud.

Under the Investments and Securities Act, 2025, only entities registered by the SEC are allowed to promote investment services, give investment advice, or collect money from the public for investment in the Nigerian capital market.

In an alert published on 30 June 2025 about an unregistered platform, the SEC listed typical signs of a fraudulent Ponzi scheme: promises of guaranteed returns, heavy use of a referral system that pays you money for bringing in other people, and desperate requests for members to keep putting money into their accounts.

In June 2025 the SEC repeated that CBEX (Crypto Bridge Exchange), also known as ST Technologies International Ltd, Smart Treasure or Super Technology, is not registered with the SEC. The SEC said media reports showed CBEX promoters were asking members to pay $200 or $100 before they could withdraw their money. The SEC advised the public not to deal with CBEX, because they risk losing their money. This example shows that some illegal platforms use more than one name.

When the SEC names a platform as unregistered, it usually says that anyone who deals with that platform does so at his or her own risk.

How to check if an investment company is registered with the SEC before you pay:
1. Go to the official SEC website, sec.gov.ng. The SEC notices point to www.sec.gov.ng/cmos, which opens the "Find a Registered Operator" page.
2. Search for the company by its exact name. Also search any other names it uses.
3. Check the scope of services the company is registered for. The directory lets you confirm a company's registration status and see its services.
4. If the company offers app-based or online investment, also check the SEC's list of registered fintech operators at https://sec.gov.ng/fintech-and-innovation-hub-finport/registered-fintech-operators/.
5. Check the SEC's enforcement updates page, where it publishes "Illegal Operator Alerts" naming platforms that are not registered.
6. If you cannot confirm that the company is registered, do not invest or send money. The SEC strongly advises the public to always confirm from the SEC that a company offering investment services is registered.

The SEC also says that even when a company is registered, investors should do their own checks and consult qualified professionals before making investment decisions.
