If you have a problem with your bank, the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) says you must complain to the bank first. Go to the bank or branch where the problem started and give them a written complaint. Ask for an acknowledgement copy and a complaint tracking number, and keep them safe. The CBN may not take your complaint if you cannot show that you complained to the bank first.

CBN guidance says you should give the bank 2 weeks {{VERIFY}} to solve the problem. For complaints about excess charges and loans, the time given is 30 days {{VERIFY}}. CBN documents from different years do not all give the same timelines, so check the current CBN guidance if the exact deadline matters.

How to take your complaint to the CBN if your bank does not solve it:
1. Wait for the bank's time limit to pass, or until the bank refuses or fails to give you a tracking number.
2. Write to the Director, Consumer Protection Department, Central Bank of Nigeria, Abuja. You can send it by email to cpd@cbn.gov.ng {{VERIFY}}, or submit a letter at the CBN Head Office or at any CBN branch.
3. Attach copies of your documents and proof that you complained to your bank first. Quote the bank's tracking number.

Your complaint letter to the CBN should include your name, address, phone number and email, the name of your bank, the date and history of the problem transaction, the amount involved, and copies of supporting documents. Never write your PIN or passwords in the letter.

The CBN says it does not charge any fee to handle your complaint. If anyone asks you to pay money so that the CBN will look at your complaint, it is not the CBN.

The CBN's Consumer Protection Department handles complaints only against institutions the CBN regulates, such as commercial banks, microfinance banks and primary mortgage institutions.

The CBN has warned the public many times about fake offers of contracts, loans, grants and intervention funds that use its name. In May 2025 {{VERIFY}} the CBN said it does not award contracts or give out funds through unsolicited emails, phone calls, text messages, WhatsApp or other social media. It said it does not ask people to pay fees to get contracts, grants or financial help, and it does not use middlemen or third parties to offer money to the public.

In April 2026 {{VERIFY}} the CBN warned that fraudsters were sending fake emails, text messages and online content that claim to come from the CBN, mostly to make people click dangerous links. The CBN said it does not ask for personal or financial information through unofficial channels. It asked the public to report suspicious messages, emails or websites to law enforcement.

The CBN's fraud awareness advice says do not open links in suspicious messages. If a message claims to be from your bank or another organisation, contact that organisation directly through a channel you already trust.

If you think you have been defrauded, the CBN advises you to:
1. Contact your bank immediately, so it can act on your account and any unauthorised transaction.
2. Report the matter to law enforcement. The CBN lists bodies such as the EFCC, the Nigeria Police Force, the DSS and the ICPC.
3. Change your passwords and turn on two-factor authentication if your personal details may have been exposed.
