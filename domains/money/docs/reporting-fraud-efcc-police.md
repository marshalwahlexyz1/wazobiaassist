If you have lost money to fraud, the Central Bank of Nigeria advises you to contact your bank immediately so it can freeze your account and reverse unauthorised transactions. Then report the fraud to authorities such as the EFCC, the Nigeria Police Force, the Department of State Services (DSS) or the ICPC.

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) enforces the laws against economic and financial crimes in Nigeria. Its website, efcc.gov.ng, has a "How to report a crime" page with these options:
1. Submit a petition online using the "Submit Petition Online" form on efcc.gov.ng.
2. Submit a written petition in person at the EFCC headquarters or at any EFCC zonal directorate. The addresses are on the "Directorate" page of the EFCC website.
3. Use the EFCC Eagle App, which the EFCC says lets you report financial crimes from your phone. It is free on Android (Google Play) and iOS (Apple App Store).

The EFCC says anyone, a person or an organisation, inside or outside Nigeria, may send a petition where there are reasonable grounds. If the person complaining cannot read or write, an EFCC officer will write down the complaint and read it back to them. A petition may include your name, contact details, address and state, a short title, and the time the offence happened.

EFCC headquarters contact details from the EFCC website: Plot 301/302, Institutions and Research Cadastral District, Jabi, Abuja (opposite the National Open University, Jabi). Phone: +234 99 044 000 or +234 809 332 2644. Email: info@efcc.gov.ng.

In October 2024 the EFCC set up a Public Complaints Desk at all its zonal directorates and its headquarters. It receives complaints from the public about the EFCC's own activities and officers. The EFCC says the desk's phone number is on its website and social media pages.

Only use EFCC contact details found on the EFCC's own website. On 21 April 2026 the EFCC warned about criminals pretending to be EFCC officers. It said some wear fake uniforms near eateries and fun spots, harass young people, accuse them of internet fraud and take their valuables. Others stop motorists and use weapons to force them to pay money. Others post fake videos of EFCC "sting operations" online.

The EFCC has also said, in a 2019 statement, that it does not mount roadblocks, does not hire agents, does not invite people online or by email, and does not use Gmail. In a 2014 statement it said that no genuine EFCC invitation letter will ask you to call an official on the phone. Instead, a real letter asks you to come to an EFCC office to be interviewed by a named officer.

The Nigeria Police Force takes reports of online fraud and other cybercrime through its National Cybercrime Centre (NPF-NCCC). In a statement dated 30 May 2024, the police said its cybercrime reporting platform is at https://nccc.npf.gov.ng/ereport/signin, and that this replaced the old site incb.npf.gov.ng, which must no longer be used. The police said the platform protects users' information and keeps reports confidential, and that it lets you track your report.

Steps after you are defrauded:
1. Contact your bank immediately and tell them what happened.
2. Report to the EFCC or to the police cybercrime portal, using only the official channels above.
3. If your personal details were exposed, change your passwords and turn on two-factor authentication.
