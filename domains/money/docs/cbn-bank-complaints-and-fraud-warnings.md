If you have a problem with your bank, the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) says you must first report it at the bank or branch where the problem started. You can walk into any branch of your bank and give them a written complaint. Collect an acknowledgement copy of your complaint and keep it. You can also complain through the bank's official phone lines and email addresses.

The CBN complaint guide says you should allow your bank 2 weeks to solve the problem (some banks may take less time). Under a revised CBN circular dated 30 June 2014, banks must solve complaints about excess charges and loans within 30 days. The CBN's consumer protection FAQs also say banks must resolve ATM dispense errors within 72 hours. These timelines come from older CBN documents, so newer CBN rules may be different.

How to take your complaint to the CBN if your bank does not solve it:
1. Wait until the 2 weeks (or 30 days for excess charges and loans) have passed, or until the bank's answer does not satisfy you.
2. Write a letter to: The Director, Consumer Protection Department, Central Bank of Nigeria, Central Business District, Abuja. You can submit the letter at the CBN Head Office or at any CBN branch.
3. Or send your complaint by email to cpd@cbn.gov.ng and attach your documents.
4. Include proof that you first complained to your bank.

Your complaint letter to the CBN should be clear and short. It should contain your name, address, phone number and email; the name of your bank; your banking details; the date and history of the transaction you are complaining about; the amount you are claiming; and copies of documents that support your claim. Do NOT write your PIN or passwords in the letter.

The CBN does not charge any fee to handle your complaint.

The CBN only handles complaints against financial institutions it regulates, such as commercial banks, microfinance banks, primary mortgage institutions and discount houses. It does not handle complaints that are already before a court.

In May 2025 the CBN warned the public again about people who falsely claim to act for it and offer fake contracts, loans, grants and intervention funds. The CBN said it has not appointed any person, group or organisation as its agent to offer contracts, grants or intervention funds to the public. It said it does not award contracts or give out money through unsolicited emails, phone calls, SMS, WhatsApp or any social media platform. It also said it does not ask for payments or fees for any contract, grant or financial help. It advised people not to engage with such offers and to report them to law enforcement agencies or the nearest CBN branch.

In April 2026 the CBN warned about fake messages, emails and online content that claim to come from the CBN. It said these messages try to make people click dangerous links and to steal personal and financial information. The CBN said its official website is www.cbn.gov.ng. It advised people not to click suspicious links or share personal details on unverified platforms, and to report suspicious messages, emails or websites to law enforcement.

The CBN's fraud awareness page advises: do not click on suspicious links in emails or messages; check who sent a message by contacting the organisation directly; and be careful when anyone asks for personal or financial information online. For investments, it warns against "get rich quick" schemes that promise high returns with little or no risk.

If you think you have been defrauded, the CBN advises you to:
1. Contact your bank immediately so it can freeze your account and reverse unauthorised transactions.
2. Report the fraud to authorities such as the EFCC, the Department of State Services (DSS), the Nigeria Police Force and the ICPC.
3. Change your passwords and turn on two-factor authentication if your personal information was exposed.

In a circular dated 12 March 2026, the CBN said customers can switch off instant transfers on their accounts for any length of time. You can still make transfers by going to the bank in person. Banks must also let customers set lower personal transaction limits. When a mobile banking app is activated on a new device, it has a temporary limit of N20,000 for the first 24 hours. The CBN said these standards take effect on 1 July 2026.
