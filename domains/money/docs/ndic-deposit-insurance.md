The Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation (NDIC) protects people who keep money in banks. If an insured bank fails, the NDIC pays each depositor their insured money, up to a maximum amount set by law. The bank pays the insurance premium. The depositor pays nothing.

All deposit-taking banks licensed by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) must be members, under the NDIC Act No 33 of 2023. These are deposit money banks (commercial banks), microfinance banks, primary mortgage banks, non-interest banks and payment service banks.

The NDIC's current maximum cover is N5,000,000 per depositor for deposit money banks and for mobile money subscribers. For microfinance banks, primary mortgage banks and payment service banks, it is N2,000,000. The NDIC announced these limits on 2 May 2024, with immediate effect. Before that, the limit was N500,000 for commercial banks, N200,000 for microfinance banks, and N500,000 for primary mortgage banks and payment service banks. Some older documents still show the old amounts.

The limit applies to each depositor in each insured bank. If you have more money in a failed bank than the limit, you do not get the extra at once.

If you use a mobile money operator, your money is held in a pool or trust account in a bank. The NDIC insures each subscriber's balance in that account up to N5,000,000, if certain conditions are met. The mobile money operator itself is not covered in this way.

Non-interest (Islamic) bank deposits are covered under a separate NDIC scheme. For that scheme, the NDIC lists insider-related deposits, interbank placements and borrowings as excluded.

When the CBN revokes a bank's licence, the NDIC becomes the liquidator. It checks depositors' records, pays insured deposits, issues liquidator certificates, and then pays "liquidation dividends" over time. Liquidation dividends are paid to depositors whose balances were above the insured limit, using money from selling the failed bank's assets and recovering its loans. Insured depositors are paid first, then uninsured depositors, then creditors, then shareholders.

On 1 July 2026 the CBN revoked the licences of 46 microfinance banks. The NDIC said depositors whose Bank Verification Numbers (BVNs) were linked to their accounts in the failed banks would be paid through their accounts in other banks. Depositors without a linked BVN were asked to visit the failed bank's office with proof of account ownership, a passport photograph, a valid ID (driver's licence, voter's card, international passport or national ID card) and their BVN. Claims could also be filed online on the NDIC website or at any NDIC office. The NDIC gave these lines for enquiries: 09037273810, 09038197064, 08104220807, 09064657140.

How to file a claim with the NDIC after your bank is closed:
1. Gather proof of your deposit (passbook, cheque book, statement of account or fixed deposit certificate) and a valid ID.
2. Get a recent passport photograph and the details of an active account in another bank for payment.
3. Fill the Depositors Verification Form online on the "Your Claims" page of ndic.gov.ng, or download it.
4. If you fill it by hand, email the scanned documents to claimscomplaints@ndic.gov.ng, or deliver them to: The Director, Claims Resolution Department, Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation, Mamman Kontagora House (MKH), 23A Marina, Lagos.
5. Make sure your name is the same on all documents.

"Wonder banks" that collect money and promise "too good to believe" returns are not licensed by the CBN and are not insured by the NDIC.
