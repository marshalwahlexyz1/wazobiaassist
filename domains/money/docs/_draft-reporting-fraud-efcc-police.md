If you have lost money to fraud, the Central Bank of Nigeria advises you to contact your bank immediately, then report the matter to law enforcement such as the EFCC or the Nigeria Police Force.

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) investigates financial crimes such as fraud and scams. You can report to the EFCC through its EagleEye app or the EagleEye website at eagleeye.efcc.gov.ng {{VERIFY}}, where you choose "Report Case". The app lets you upload evidence. You can also write to the EFCC at info@efcc.gov.ng {{VERIFY}}, or go in person to the EFCC headquarters in Jabi, Abuja, or to any EFCC zonal office.

The EFCC has set up a Public Complaints Desk at its zonal offices and its headquarters to receive complaints from the public. The EFCC says the phone number of the desk is published on its website and official social media accounts.

Only use EFCC contact details that you find on the EFCC's own website, efcc.gov.ng. In April 2026 {{VERIFY}} the EFCC warned about a rise in people pretending to be EFCC officers. It advised the public to verify suspicious activities and report them through the Commission's official channels.

A genuine EFCC invitation letter will not ask you to call an officer on the phone. Instead, it asks you to come to an EFCC office to be interviewed by a named officer.

The Nigeria Police Force reports online fraud and other cybercrime through the Police National Cybercrime Centre (NPF-NCCC). The police say cybercrime reports should be made on the NPF-NCCC eReporting portal at https://nccc.npf.gov.ng/ereport/signin {{VERIFY}}. The police said this website replaces the older site incb.npf.gov.ng, which should no longer be used. The portal allows confidential reports.

Steps after you are defrauded:
1. Contact your bank immediately and tell them what happened.
2. Report to the EFCC or the Nigeria Police Force using only the official channels above.
