The Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation (NDIC) insures money that people keep in licensed banks. If an insured bank fails, the NDIC pays depositors their insured money up to a maximum amount. Depositors do not need to apply or pay for this cover. Eligible deposit accounts are insured automatically at no charge.

Savings accounts, current accounts and time or fixed deposits in insured institutions are covered.

In May 2024 {{VERIFY}} the NDIC raised its maximum cover. The NDIC now says it covers up to N5 million {{VERIFY}} per depositor in deposit money banks (commercial banks) and for subscribers of mobile money operators. It covers up to N2 million {{VERIFY}} per depositor in microfinance banks, primary mortgage banks and payment service banks. Some older NDIC pages and other guides still show the old, lower limits, so check the NDIC website for the current figures.

The limit is per depositor, per bank, not per account. All your accounts in the same bank, including its branches, are added together and covered up to the limit. Accounts in different banks are covered separately. Opening many accounts in the same bank does not increase your cover.

Some deposits are not insured. For microfinance banks and primary mortgage institutions, the NDIC lists exclusions such as deposits of the bank's own board members, management and staff (insider deposits), and deposits used as security for a loan.

The NDIC pays insured deposits only when the CBN closes the bank or when the bank suspends payments.

If you have more than the insured limit in a failed bank, the extra money is not paid at once. It may be paid later as "liquidation dividends" from money the NDIC gets by selling the failed bank's assets and recovering its loans. Depositors are paid before creditors and shareholders.

The NDIC uses your Bank Verification Number (BVN) to find your other bank accounts and pay your insured money into them. This is faster when your BVN is linked to your account in the failed bank and you have another account in a working bank in the same name. Depositors without a BVN or another account have been among those left waiting.

In July 2026 {{VERIFY}} the CBN revoked the licences of 46 microfinance banks {{VERIFY}}. The NDIC said depositors whose BVNs were linked to their accounts would be paid through their accounts in other banks. Those without a linked BVN were told to visit the affected bank's office with proof of account ownership, a passport photograph, valid ID and their BVN. Claims can also be made online or at any NDIC office.
