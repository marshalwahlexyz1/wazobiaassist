Source: WazobiaAssist scam patterns (from CBN, SEC and FCCPC warnings)

Fake 'BVN/NIN/account blocked' message. Warning signs: Says your BVN, NIN, SIM or bank account is blocked, suspended or needs update. Asks you to click a link, call a number or reply with details. Creates urgency (e.g. 'within 24 hours'). What to do: Do not click the link or call the number in the message. Contact your bank only through its official app or the number on the back of your card.

Request for OTP, PIN, password or card details. Warning signs: Caller or message claims to be from a bank, CBN, NIBSS, telco or 'customer care' and asks for your OTP, PIN, password, CVV, full card number or token code. What to do: Banks never ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Hang up. Never share these codes with anyone.

'Wrong transfer, please send it back'. Warning signs: Someone says they sent money to you by mistake and begs you to send it back, often with a credit alert SMS or screenshot. The money is usually not really in your account. What to do: Check your balance in your bank app, not the SMS. If money really arrived by mistake, let your bank reverse it. Do not send money yourself.

Fake credit alert or payment screenshot. Warning signs: A buyer shows a payment screenshot or you get an SMS credit alert, but the balance in your bank app has not changed. Common against traders and POS operators. What to do: Only release goods after the money shows in your bank app or USSD balance.

Ponzi / 'double your money' investment. Warning signs: Promises fixed high returns (e.g. 20-50% per month), guaranteed profit, daily earnings, referral bonuses, crypto 'mining', trading robots, or 'task' apps where you pay to unlock earnings. Spread on WhatsApp, Telegram, TikTok. What to do: Guaranteed high returns are a warning sign. Only SEC-registered companies may collect investments from the public; check the SEC Nigeria website before paying anything.

Farm or business 'sponsorship' with fixed returns. Warning signs: Invites you to 'sponsor' poultry, cattle, farmland, real estate or a business for a fixed high return in a few months. What to do: Treat fixed high returns as a Ponzi warning sign. Verify SEC registration and visit the real business before paying.

Fake job offer with a fee. Warning signs: Job, recruitment or 'work from home' offer that asks for a registration, training, uniform, medical or processing fee before you start. Often uses big company or government names. What to do: Real employers do not charge you to get a job. Do not pay. Confirm on the company's official website.

Fake government grant, palliative or empowerment fund. Warning signs: Says you qualify for an FG, state, CBN, NGO or 'empowerment' grant, loan or cash transfer, but you must pay a fee or share your bank details/BVN first. Often a link to a form. What to do: Government programmes do not ask you to pay to receive money. Check the agency's official website or social media accounts.

Fake promo, lottery or prize. Warning signs: Says you won a promo, prize, car or money from a telco, bank or brand you did not enter, and asks for a 'delivery', 'tax' or 'activation' fee, or your details. What to do: You cannot win a promo you did not enter. Never pay a fee to collect a prize.

Romance or 'gift stuck at customs'. Warning signs: An online friend or lover, often abroad, sends a gift or money that is 'held at customs/airport', and someone asks you to pay clearance fees. Or the friend asks you for money for an emergency. What to do: Do not pay clearance fees for gifts from people you have not met. This is a common romance scam.

Friend or relative urgently asking for money. Warning signs: A message from a friend's or relative's account (WhatsApp, Facebook) asks for an urgent transfer, often to a different person's account, and avoids calls. What to do: Call the person on their usual number before sending anything. Their account may have been hacked.

'Send me the code sent to your phone'. Warning signs: Someone asks you to forward a 6-digit code that was sent to your phone by SMS, often saying it was sent to you by mistake. What to do: Never share codes sent to your phone. That code lets them take over your WhatsApp or account. Turn on WhatsApp two-step verification.

Fake customer care on social media. Warning signs: After you complain publicly about a bank, telco or app, an account DMs you claiming to be customer care and asks for details, a link click, or remote access app. What to do: Only use support channels listed in the official app or website. Real support will not ask for your PIN or OTP.

Illegal loan app. Warning signs: Loan app asks for an upfront 'processing fee', demands access to your contacts or photos, charges very high interest, or threatens to shame you to your contacts. What to do: Avoid loan apps that charge fees before paying out or want your contacts. Use FCCPC-approved lenders only. Report harassment to the FCCPC.

Online seller who wants full payment first. Warning signs: Seller on Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp or Jiji asks for full payment before delivery, price is far below market, refuses pay-on-delivery or meeting, pressures you to pay now. What to do: Prefer pay-on-delivery or meet in a safe public place. Be careful with prices that are too good to be true.

Fake visa, scholarship or travel agent. Warning signs: Guarantees a visa, job abroad or scholarship for a fee, asks for payment into a personal account, or uses unofficial email addresses. What to do: No agent can guarantee a visa. Use official embassy and university websites. Do not pay into personal accounts.

Fake police, EFCC or official demanding money. Warning signs: Caller claims to be police, EFCC, court or another agency, says you are under investigation or your account is linked to a crime, and asks for money or details to 'settle' it. What to do: Real agencies do not settle cases by phone transfer. Hang up and contact the agency through its official channels.

Suspicious link. Warning signs: Short links (bit.ly etc.), misspelled bank or brand names, links ending in odd domains, or pages asking you to log in or enter card details after clicking from a message. What to do: Do not log in through links in messages. Open your bank or app directly instead.

SIM swap or SIM registration call. Warning signs: Caller says your SIM will be blocked unless you 'verify' it, asks for your NIN, BVN, or codes, or your phone suddenly loses network after such a call. What to do: Do not share your details. If your SIM suddenly stops working, contact your network provider and your bank at once.

What genuine messages usually look like (to avoid false alarms). Warning signs: A real bank debit/credit alert shows a transaction you recognise and matches your app balance. An OTP you requested yourself while logging in or paying is normal. Genuine messages do not ask you to reply with a PIN or OTP, click to 'unblock' an account, or pay a fee to receive money. What to do: If the message matches something you did yourself and asks for nothing, it is probably genuine. If unsure, check in your official app.
